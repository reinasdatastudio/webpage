From Pharmacy to Data Science: Why I Chose R

My journey from pharmacy to data science—and why R became my go-to language for reproducible analysis.
Published

February 12, 2026

When people see that I started my career in pharmacy and now work in data science, the first question is usually:

“How did that happen?”

The short answer is curiosity.

The longer answer is that I’ve always been drawn to understanding systems — whether that system is the human body or a dataset.

Starting in Pharmacy

Pharmacy taught me something that still shapes how I work today: precision matters.

In healthcare, small details are not small. A misplaced decimal, a misunderstood value, or an incorrect assumption can have serious consequences. You learn to check carefully, to document properly, and to understand what each number actually represents.

But over time, I realised that what I enjoyed most was not memorising information — it was analysing it.

I enjoyed understanding patterns.

I enjoyed interpreting results.

I enjoyed asking: Why does this happen?

That curiosity eventually led me toward computer science.

Discovering Programming

While studying engineering at the University of Auckland, I discovered computer science — and immediately fell in love with programming.

Programming felt different. It was logical, structured, and creative at the same time. It rewarded curiosity and precision. It gave immediate feedback. Most importantly, it felt like something I could continuously grow in.

I later completed a one-year computer science programme with a focus on data science, where I was introduced more deeply to R.

And that’s where something clicked.

Why R?

R stood out to me for several reasons.

First, it was built for statistics and analysis. It wasn’t just a general programming language — it was designed for thinking about data.

Second, it encouraged reproducibility. Tools like R Markdown and Quarto made it possible to combine analysis, interpretation, and reporting in a single, transparent workflow. That felt aligned with the healthcare mindset I came from.

Third, it made complex analysis accessible. With the right structure, R allows you to move from raw data to meaningful insight in a clear and systematic way.

To me, R felt like a bridge between:

  • clinical precision
  • statistical reasoning
  • and structured programming

It wasn’t just about writing code. It was about creating clarity.

From Employment to Independence

After transitioning into data science, I worked in system engineering to broaden my technical experience while continuing to develop my data skills.

Eventually, I realised that I wanted to focus fully on what I enjoyed most: working directly with data and helping others understand it.

Starting Reina’s Data Studio was a natural step. It allowed me to:

  • Work independently
  • Support researchers and healthcare professionals
  • Share my enthusiasm for R
  • Build something aligned with my values

How Pharmacy Still Shapes My Work

My healthcare background continues to influence how I approach every project.

I don’t treat data as abstract numbers. I think about:

  • What does this variable really represent?
  • Where did this data come from?
  • What assumptions are we making?
  • Can this analysis be reproduced and audited later?

That mindset makes me careful — but also practical. Clean data. Clear documentation. Transparent decisions.

In healthcare and research, that matters.

Teaching and Sharing

One of my core values is continuous learning. I genuinely believe that knowledge never betrays you.

That belief is why I share tutorials on YouTube and write about R here. Many R resources are in English, and I enjoy helping both English and Japanese speakers feel more confident navigating data and programming.

R is constantly evolving. I am, too.

Looking Forward

Reina’s Data Studio is built on three things:

  • Transparency

  • Precision

  • A genuine passion for learning

Whether I’m debugging a script, building a reproducible report, or explaining a statistical result, my goal is always the same:

To help people work with data clearly, ethically, and confidently.
